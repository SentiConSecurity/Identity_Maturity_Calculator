# Maturity Levels

The assessment scores every question on a common 0–5 scale, adapted from the SEI Capability Maturity Model Integration (CMMI) framework.

| Score | Level Name | CMM Level | Description |
|:-:|---|---|---|
| 0 | Non Existent | Level 0 – Incomplete | No process or control exists for this capability. There is no awareness of the need for it, no activity performed, and no accountability assigned. This level falls below the formal CMM scale and represents an absence of any managed practice. |
| 1 | Ad Hoc | Level 1 – Initial | Processes exist but are unpredictable, poorly controlled, and reactive. Success depends on individual heroics rather than repeatable practice. There is no formal documentation, planning, or governance. Results are inconsistent and difficult to replicate. |
| 2 | Developing | Level 2 – Managed | Basic project or process management is in place. Requirements are managed, and processes are planned, performed, measured, and controlled at the project level. Practices may not be consistent across the organization, but there is intent to standardize. |
| 3 | Defined | Level 3 – Defined | Processes are well-characterized, documented, and standardized across the organization. A defined process is tailored from the organization's set of standard processes. There is proactive sharing of lessons learned and a common understanding of activities, roles, and responsibilities. |
| 4 | Managed | Level 4 – Quantitatively Managed | Quantitative objectives for quality and process performance are established and used as criteria for managing processes. Statistical and other quantitative techniques are used to understand performance variation and to predict future outcomes. Sub-process performance is understood in statistical terms. |
| 5 | Optimized | Level 5 – Optimizing | Focus is on continual process improvement through incremental and innovative improvements. The organization uses quantitative understanding of its processes to drive improvement decisions. Causes of defects and process variation are identified and eliminated. The organization is agile and able to adapt rapidly to change and new opportunities. |

## Interpreting the Scale

A useful way to think about the scale in identity security terms:

- **0–1** generally means the capability is either absent or entirely dependent on individual effort, with no institutional memory or repeatability.
- **2–3** generally means the organization has moved from ad hoc to structured, but consistency across the whole enterprise (not just one team, one application, or one platform) is the differentiator between a 2 and a 3.
- **4–5** generally means the capability is not just present and consistent, but actively measured, managed against metrics, and improved over time — the hallmark of a mature program rather than a mature project.

## Customizing the Scale

This scale, like the rest of the assessment, is intentionally adaptable. If your organization's risk appetite or maturity expectations differ from the defaults above, feel free to adjust the level descriptions to better suit your needs. For example, if you believe a particular threshold used elsewhere in your organization's maturity models (e.g. an acceptable policy exception rate, or an SLA for privileged session review) should map differently to a given level, change the description on the **Maturity Levels** tab to reflect that. What matters most is that the same interpretation is then applied consistently across all 9 domains and all 36 questions — inconsistent interpretation of the scale is one of the most common ways an assessment loses credibility with stakeholders.

## Why 0–5 and Not a Different Range

The 0–5 range was chosen for two practical reasons. First, it maps directly onto the six CMMI maturity levels (0 through 5), so no translation is needed between "the framework" and "the score you enter." Second, a six-point scale is granular enough to show meaningful movement between reassessment cycles, without being so granular (e.g. a 1–10 or percentage scale) that scorers spend more time debating precision than substance.
